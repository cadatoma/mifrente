## 1. Are the plan's two "why now" claims true? (Topaz → subscription Oct 2025; Adobe agreed to buy Topaz June 2026)

### Takeaway
Both claims are essentially **true**, but the plan (dated Oct 19, 2026) is **out of date**. Adobe did not just "agree" to buy Topaz: it **completed** the purchase on **Sept 23, 2026**. Topaz stays a standalone brand, and no price changes have been announced. Topaz models have also been inside Photoshop since **Oct 2025** (Adobe MAX), so the "Topaz as a way to avoid Adobe" story had already weakened before the deal. The plan's cited source (PhotoWorkout) is secondary; primary sources exist and should replace it.

### Inferences
- The plan should say "Adobe completed the purchase of Topaz on Sept 23, 2026", not "agreed in June". The plan is dated Oct 19, nearly a month after the close, so the error makes the plan look under-researched.
- The "why now" story is real but weaker than the plan suggests:
  - Topaz's switch to subscription is a year old (Oct 2025). Most users who wanted a perpetual alternative have had 12 months to move, and DxO, ON1 and Pixelmator Pro have been there to catch them.
  - The Adobe close removes some uncertainty, and nothing has changed for users so far. The real trigger would be a future Topaz price increase or forced Creative Cloud bundling, and neither has happened.
- Perpetual Topaz owners still have working apps. They are a "no new models" crowd, not a stranded one.

### Gaps
- I could not read the Adobe newsroom or Topaz pages in full (fetching was blocked), so exact quotes, any regulatory filing details and any SEC 8-K were not checked. Adobe may not have filed an 8-K for a deal of this size.
- I could not confirm Topaz's current official price list with certainty: snippets conflict between $99 and $149/yr for Gigapixel Personal, and a promo was running.
- No reliable data on how many Topaz users have churned since Oct 2025.

### Inferences
- The "$59, one-time, on-device, Mac" niche is **already crowded**:
  - Pixelmator Pro costs $10 less, comes from Apple, is on the Mac App Store and does far more.
  - ON1 NoNoise AI 2027 launched three weeks ago, at the same launch price as the plan ($49.99) and with the same "no subscription" message, plus a plugin.
  - Aiarty sells a lifetime Mac licence near $69 with constant promotions.
  - Upscayl is free for upscaling.
- The plan's MVP leaves out Lightroom/Photoshop plugins **and DNG output** (it outputs JPEG, 16-bit TIFF and PNG). For RAW shooters, DNG output that drops back into Lightroom is how PureRAW and ON1 fit into the workflow. Without it, the app is a step backwards for the plan's stated target user: wedding, event and sports pros.
- Lightroom's denoise is **RAW-only**. That leaves a real (if small) opening for **JPEG/HEIC/TIFF denoise**: phone photos, old JPEG archives, out-of-camera JPEGs from event shooters. Pixelmator Pro, Aiarty and Topaz already cover it.

## 3. Best-in-class per task (RAW high-ISO denoise; upscale for print/crops; sharpen/motion blur)

### Takeaway
- **RAW denoise:** DxO (DeepPRIME XD2/XD3) is the most common #1, Topaz and Lightroom are close behind, and ON1 trails on quality but is cheapest.
- **Upscale and sharpen:** Topaz (Gigapixel, Photo with Super Focus and Wonder) is the reference. Adobe now ships Topaz Gigapixel inside Photoshop.
- An app built on generic open-source models will start **below** all of these on RAW denoise.

### Cited Findings
- Most 2026 roundups name **DxO PureRAW 6** the best dedicated noise-reduction tool and the cleanest on high-ISO files, with an edge above ISO 12,800. **Topaz** is described as sometimes the most natural, but able to invent detail or leave an artificial texture. **Lightroom** is a close third and slightly softer at high ISO, with convenience as its main advantage. **ON1 NoNoise** is cheaper but a step behind — [Capture the Atlas](https://capturetheatlas.com/noise-reduction-software/); [Finding the Universe](https://www.findingtheuniverse.com/best-noise-reduction-software/); [ePHOTOzine](https://www.ephotozine.com/article/comparing-6-best-ai-noise-reduction-software-for-low-light-photography-37522); [Markus Hagner, Sept 2026](https://markus-hagner-photography.com/topaz-photo-ai-vs-dxo-pureraw-for-noise-reduction/). These are mostly independent blogs, and their rankings vary.
- Fstoppers ran a multi-tool comparison of AI noise reduction ("Pixel Perfect or Problematic?") — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/software/pixel-perfect-or-problematic-best-ai-noise-reduction-software-699404). Its result was not read because fetching was blocked.
- Photography Life's test of DxO DeepPRIME XD calls it an evolution of noise reduction. The test predates 2025 — [Photography Life](https://photographylife.com/reviews/dxo-deepprime-xd).
- On DeepPRIME XD3 versus XD2: one reviewer saw little visible improvement, another measured it roughly 20–25% faster with less aggressive default smoothing — [Digital Camera World](https://www.digitalcameraworld.com/photography/photo-editing/dxo-pureraw-5-review); [Life after Photoshop](https://lifeafterphotoshop.com/dxo-pureraw-5-review/).
- On upscaling, Adobe itself positions **Topaz Gigapixel** as the "preserve existing detail" option and Bloom as the creative or generative one inside Photoshop — [Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/photoshop/using/enhance-image-quality-with-generative-upscale.html).
- One 2026 blog compares an open model (**NAFNet**) directly with Topaz, Lightroom and DxO — [ArtImageHub](https://artimagehub.com/blog/ai-photo-denoiser-comparison-2026). This is low-authority but relevant to the plan's model choice. Its conclusions were not read.
- Lightroom's Super Resolution is 2x per pass with no face reconstruction and no deblur for missed focus — [Krea](https://www.krea.ai/blog/best-ai-photo-enhancers-in-2026).

### Inferences
- The plan's models (SCUNet, NAFNet, Real-ESRGAN, SwinIR) work on already-rendered RGB images. DxO's lead comes from denoising **at the RAW or demosaic stage**, which is why the "RAW input" bullet in the plan does not by itself make the app competitive on RAW denoise.
  - This is an inference from the general architecture, not something cited above.
  - The week-2 go/no-go test should include **DxO PureRAW 6's trial** and **ON1 NoNoise 2027**, not only Lightroom and Topaz.
- On sharpening, Topaz's Super Focus and noise-aware Sharpen set a bar that "light sharpen" will not reach. Positioning it as a minor extra is the right call.

### Gaps
- I did not reach YouTube comparison titles or the full PetaPixel, DPReview or Fstoppers test results (fetching was blocked).
- I found no 2025–2026 head-to-head test of motion-blur or sharpening tools, and no comparison of Aiarty against the leaders from an independent major outlet.

## 4. What Apple gives for free or cheaply (the floor)

### Takeaway
Apple now owns Pixelmator and sells Pixelmator Pro for **$49.99 one-time**, with ML Denoise and ML Super Resolution. It is also bundled in **Apple Creator Studio ($12.99/mo, $129/yr)**. That sets an effective floor just below the plan's $59, from the platform owner itself, with Mac App Store prominence. Free options also exist: Affinity (no AI denoise found) and Upscayl (free upscaling).

### Cited Findings
- Pixelmator Pro is part of Apple Creator Studio (launched Jan 28 2026; $12.99/mo or $129/yr; education $2.99/mo or $29.99/yr; one-month free trial). It is "also available as a one-time purchase on the App Store" — [Apple](https://www.apple.com/pixelmator-pro/); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/01/28/apple-creator-studio-hands-on/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/13/apple-launches-creator-studio-bundle-of-apps-for-12-99-per-month); [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3031215/apples-new-creator-studio-is-the-pro-subscription-bundle-weve-been-waiting-for.html).
- Pixelmator Pro's Denoise removes camera noise and JPEG artefacts, and Super Resolution "automatically scales up the resolution so it stays sharp". It supports 750+ RAW formats — [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/guide/pixelmator-pro/automatically-reduce-image-noise-pixb11dfe403/mac); [Apple](https://www.apple.com/pixelmator-pro/).
- Some newer Pixelmator Pro capabilities (such as Warp) are reportedly tied to Creator Studio even for owners of the perpetual Mac app. This comes from a third-party source — [costbench](https://costbench.com/software/design/pixelmator-pro/).
- Affinity has been free since Oct 29 2025. Its AI features (Canva AI) require Canva Premium — [ProVideo Coalition](https://www.provideocoalition.com/affinity-new-version-is-free-forever-for-everyone/).
- Upscayl is a free, open-source Real-ESRGAN upscaler for macOS 12+ — [smolmac](https://smolmac.com/blog/best-ai-image-upscaler-mac).

### Inferences
- A $59 app has to justify costing **more than Apple's own $49.99 AI editor**. The arguments that hold up are better RAW denoise quality, a much simpler batch workflow and specialised output. Price is not one of them.
- Apple may add Pixelmator ML features to Photos. That would lower the floor further. This is speculation, with no source.

### Gaps

## 5. Is there a winnable gap at ~$59 one-time for on-device, Mac-only, simple batch denoise+upscale?

### Takeaway
**Not as the plan describes it.** The price-and-no-subscription spot is taken: Pixelmator Pro at $49.99, ON1 NoNoise at $69.99/$49.99, Aiarty at ~$69–109 lifetime, Luminar Neo at ~$79, and Upscayl free. The quality leader also sells one-time (DxO at $139.99). A defensible wedge would need to be **narrower**:
- extremely fast, dead-simple **batch** processing of mixed JPEG/HEIC/RAW for event shooters;
- **JPEG/HEIC denoise**, which Lightroom cannot do;
- privacy and offline as a hard guarantee;
- a **DNG/TIFF round-trip into Lightroom** (an "Edit In" or export plugin).

It should be benchmarked against DxO and ON1, not only Topaz.
